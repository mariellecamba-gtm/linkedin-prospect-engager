# About me

**Replace everything below with your own facts, then delete this line.**

This file is the only set of facts a drafted comment is allowed to claim about
you. If it is not written here and not in the prospect's post, the draft cannot
say it. That is the whole safety mechanism: a comment posted under your name that
turns out to be false is the worst thing this tool can do to you.

Keep it current. Adding a real project here immediately makes every comment more
specific, and deleting a stale one stops the tool repeating it.

Aim for 30 to 60 lines. More context is better than less.

## Who I am

- Your name, your role, your company, and what the company actually does.
- The one-line version of how you got here. Previous roles count, they are what
  make a comment on someone else's career post honest.
- Where you are based, if it ever matters.

## What I actually work with, day to day

- The tools you genuinely use, by name. This is the most valuable section in the
  file: naming a real tool is what lets a comment be concrete instead of generic.
- The parts of the job you do yourself versus the parts you buy.
- Anything you have built, shipped or broken recently.

## What I have an actual opinion about

- Two or three things you would say out loud in a meeting.
- Opinions are what make a comment sound like a person rather than a summary.
- Include the unflashy ones. "Most of this category is a wrapper on a data source"
  is a better line than anything complimentary.

## What I must never claim

- Named client results, revenue numbers or case studies, unless you list the ones
  you are actually allowed to say. Anything not listed here cannot be claimed.
- Having used a tool you have not used. If a post is about a tool that is absent
  from this file, the draft asks about it instead of pretending familiarity.
- Having met the prospect before.
